Biden-Xi Summit

The hashtag co-occurrence network forms two major clusters. The central cluster, containing hashtags like #china, #biden, #xijinping, #america, #americans, #fentanyl, and #coronavirus, reflects tweets focused on U.S.–China relations, public health, and political tensions. A second cluster highlights human-rights concerns, with hashtags such as #uyghurs, #uyghurgenocide, #tibetans, and #humanrights, suggesting that some users framed the summit around China’s human-rights record. Smaller, more isolated tags such as #ccp, #us, and #taiwan indicate side conversations, while still being loosely connected to the broader geopolitical context.

The user-mention network is fragmented into small, loosely connected clusters. This indicates that discussion was not dominated by a single highly connected group but instead by multiple independent sub-communities. One cluster includes journalists and policy analysts (such as @politico, @nahaltoosi, @anderscorr) discussing diplomatic implications. Another cluster centers around NBA-related accounts (such as @eneskanter, @washwizards, @nba), hinting at conversations involving sports and human rights. Smaller branches like @foxbusiness → @morningsmaria reflect mentions within media commentary. Overall, the network shows decentralized discourse with several separate conversation spheres rather than one cohesive discussion.

The first wordcloud highlights the dominant themes of early U.S. presidential rhetoric, taken from early inaugural speeches (≤1826). As evidenced by their size in the word cloud, words like government, public, nation, people, states, union, and peace appear most frequently, emphasizing foundational concerns of establishing national unity, governance, and public duty. Other prominent words - including rights, powers, commerce, constitution, and citizens - reflect a strong focus on institutional structure, civic responsibility, and national identity as the US as an independent republic was still developing and defining early values.

The comparison wordcloud reveals distinct rhetorical patterns between the two presidents, Obama and Trump. Obama’s speeches emphasize collective responsibility and aspirational themes, reflected in words like us, must, generation, work, journey, and enduring. His language focuses more on unity, progress, and shared national challenges. Trump’s speeches attempt to garner national pride and involve action-oriented messaging, shown through words such as thank, america, country, great, president, bring, dreams, and going. His rhetoric places stronger emphasis on nationalism, achievement, and forward momentum. Overall, Obama’s tone is more inclusive and reflective, while Trump’s is more assertive and rooted in nationalism.

In the lexincal dispersion plot, the term “american” appears consistently across presidential inaugural speeches, but with varying emphasis. Earlier speeches from the 1950s–1970s show sporadic use, while later speeches from Reagan through Biden display more frequent and dense mentions. This suggests an increasing rhetorical focus on national identity and appeals to “the American people” in modern presidencies. Trump’s two speeches show especially high and frequent usage, indicating an intensified emphasis on nationalistic language.

The keyness plot shows which words are significantly more characteristic of each president’s inaugural speech. This shows similar patterns as those in the comparison wordcloud, as discussed above.

What is Wordfish?

Wordfish is an unsupervised text analysis model used to measure the political position or ideology of documents based on the words they use. It works by counting how often each word appears in each document and finding patterns in those word frequencies. This tool is widely used in political science to analyze parliamentary speeches, manifestos, debates, budget speeches, and more.
